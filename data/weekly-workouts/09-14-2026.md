**Important structural note: there is no recovery between the 1K@10K
and 2K@MP within a rep** — per the spec, the 2' walk falls *between the
four 3K reps*, not between the two paces inside one. So each rep is a
continuous 3K effort: sharp-pace kilometer flowing directly into a
goal-pace 2K with zero break in between.

The 10K portions ran slower than their specific target (6:00-6:09/mi
vs. 5:50/mi, ~4% slow) and at lower HR than the MP kilometer that
followed (rep 2: 158→168; rep 3: 159→172; rep 4: 158→175) — but that HR
ordering isn't clean evidence of anything on its own. With zero
recovery between the two paces inside a rep, HR climbing from the first
kilometer into the second is just normal cardiovascular drift over a
continuous effort — it would look this way whether the leadoff K was
paced conservatively on purpose or simply didn't hit the sharper gear.
Both are plausible explanations and the data here doesn't cleanly
separate them. What *is* clear: the intended pace order held (10K
portion faster in absolute terms than the MP portion in every rep,
6:00-6:09/mi vs. 6:20-6:25/mi) even if the gap was smaller than
prescribed (~15-19 sec/mi actual vs. ~39 sec/mi intended). Terrain
doesn't factor in either way — all 4 leadoff kilometers were flat to
slightly downhill.

**Recovery walks:** ~130-150m each over the 2' window, HR staying
elevated (avg 140-161, max 164-183) — expected, 2 minutes isn't enough
for full recovery between reps this hard.

**Cool-down:** 3.37mi in 30:05 → **8:56/mi** vs. Recovery Runs target
7:43-8:27/mi — notably slower than target (one mile included a ~2min
watch-detected pause), unremarkable after a demanding session and
doesn't drive the verdict. This long cool-down (vs. the ~1mi that would
have hit the planned 10mi total) is the main reason the day ran to
12.72mi instead of 10mi.

**Heat (Hadley score):** run spanned local hours 5-7am (05:35–07:30).
Hour 5: 79.2°F/73.6°F dew → Hadley 152.8. Hour 6: 78.4°F/73.9°F dew →
Hadley 152.3. Hour 7: 78.1°F/73.8°F dew → Hadley 151.9. All in the
**151–160 band (4.5%–6.0% expected slowdown)**.

**Heat vs. observed pace:** the 10K portions' ~4% average slowdown (14
sec/mi) is numerically smaller than what the 151-160 Hadley band alone
predicts (4.5-6.0%, i.e. 15.75-21 sec/mi) — so heat could fully account
for a miss this size. But the HR comparison used above to argue against
a heat explanation doesn't hold up either, for the same reason noted
above (HR ordering across a continuous, unbroken effort isn't a clean
signal either way). Net: heat *could* explain the 10K-portion miss, but
it isn't proven by this data — same as the pacing explanation. The MP
portions, at least, are unambiguous: **faster** than target despite the
same heat, under genuine hard effort (HR up to 186, this cycle's
highest recorded) — a clear instance of beating heat-adjusted
expectations.

**Overall verdict: a strong session, with one open question.** The MP
work is unambiguous — faster than target on all 4 reps, HR climbing
assertively rather than fading, up to this cycle's highest recorded
(186). The 10K portions missed their specific target by about 4%;
whether that's heat, deliberate pacing given the no-recovery structure,
or just not hitting the sharper gear isn't something this data can
cleanly settle. Either way, the pace order was right (fast K, then
goal-pace 2K) and the session's harder, more important work — the MP
segments — was executed well.
