## Thursday 09/24 — Medium Long Run, "Mini Whopper" (Teton County, Day 1 at altitude)

**Overall:** 13.03mi (20,963.73m) in 98:46, avg HR **160** (max 188,
min 80). Planned: 13mi total, warm-up + main set both @ EZ Runs
6:30-7:29/mi (sea-level target — **not altitude-adjusted**, since only
Sunday's long run was rebuilt with the +15s adjustment), cool-down @
Recovery Runs 7:43-8:27/mi.

| Phase | Distance | Pace | Target | Avg→Max HR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | 4.02mi | 7:51/mi | 6:30-7:29/mi | 153→173 |
| Main set (+ 5x100 strides) | 8.01mi | 7:26/mi | 6:30-7:29/mi | 167→188 |
| Cool-down | 1.00mi | 7:40/mi | 7:43-8:27/mi | 141→151 |

Distance landed almost exactly on the 13mi target. Pace was slightly
slow on the warm-up, right at the fast edge of target on the main set,
and just outside (faster than) the intended recovery-pace cool-down.
**The real story is HR, not pace:** an avg HR of 160 — climbing to a
main-set average of 167 and a peak of 188 — for what were, on paper,
easy/EZ-pace efforts is a large mismatch. This is the clearest evidence
this week of the altitude cost we discussed in advance: the same
paces you'd run comfortably at sea level cost meaningfully more
cardiovascular effort here, especially on day 1 at elevation before any
acclimation. Nothing here reads as fitness-related — it's exactly the
pattern altitude predicts.

**Pace execution was excellent** — the MP+10" and 6mi-MP blocks landed
within 1 second of the altitude-adjusted target, and the closing MP/MP-5"

This is exactly the outcome we were aiming for when we rebuilt this

**But the physiological cost was severe.** Average HR of 171 for a
2:23 run, climbing to 178-187 average (peaking 194-195) through every

near or above typical threshold/max-effort HR territory for a "long run
with MP work," not a controlled aerobic effort. `workout_feel` 25
("Bad") and `workout_rpe` 8/10 confirm this matched how it felt, not
just what the watch measured. This is the clearest data point yet that
**+15s/mi undercorrected for the true altitude cost** of hitting these
paces on day 4 at elevation, on top of an already-heavy week (Thu's
13-mile medium-long + Friday's easy day preceding it) — the pace number
was right, but the effort required to hit it was harder than intended.

**No knee or Achilles issues reported** during this session or the
week — worth noting given the effort level and this runner's injury
history this cycle.

## What stands out

- **Distance and pace discipline were outstanding all week**, including
  nailing a re-engineered, altitude-adjusted long run almost to the
  second on several segments.
- **The real finding is the HR/effort gap, not the paces.** Every day
  in Jackson showed meaningfully elevated HR relative to what the same
  paces would produce at sea level — worst on Thursday (day 1) and
  Sunday (the week's hardest session, day 4), better on Saturday (day
  3), consistent with a real but incomplete acclimation curve across
  the stay.
- **The +15s/mi altitude adjustment got the pace right but not the
  effort right** — Sunday's long run hit its targets almost exactly
  while running at RPE 8/10 and HR near-max for extended stretches.
  Worth remembering for any future altitude training blocks: pace
  adjustments alone don't fully offset altitude's cardiovascular cost,
  especially stacked on cumulative volume mid-stay.
- **No pain/injury flares reported** despite the week's difficulty — a
  genuinely good sign given this runner's history this cycle.
